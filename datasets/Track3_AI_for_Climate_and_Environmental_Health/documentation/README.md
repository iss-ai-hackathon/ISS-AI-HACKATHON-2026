# Track 3: AI for Climate and Environmental Health

## Overview

Welcome to **Track 3: AI for Climate and Environmental Health** of the **ISS 2026 AI Hackathon**.

This track challenges participants to develop Artificial Intelligence (AI) solutions that integrate climate, environmental, geospatial, and demographic data to address public health challenges in Nigeria.

Unlike the other tracks, participants will work with multiple publicly available datasets, integrate them into a unified analytical dataset, engineer relevant features, and develop an AI model capable of predicting or explaining climate-sensitive public health risks.

---

## Challenge Statement

Develop an AI solution that uses climate and environmental data to predict or assess a public health outcome relevant to Nigeria.

Examples include:

* Malaria Risk Prediction
* Cholera Hotspot Prediction
* Flood Vulnerability Assessment
* Heat Stress Prediction
* Environmental Health Surveillance
* Climate-sensitive Disease Forecasting

Teams may propose another climate-health application, provided they clearly justify its public health significance.

---

## Data Sources

Participants are expected to download and integrate datasets from the official sources listed in the **Data Acquisition Guide**.

Required datasets include:

* NASA POWER Climate Data
* CHIRPS Rainfall Data
* WorldPop Population Data
* OpenStreetMap
* SRTM Digital Elevation Model (DEM)

Optional dataset:

* MODIS NDVI (Vegetation Index)

Please refer to **Data_Acquisition_Guide.pdf** for detailed instructions and download links.

---

## Expected Workflow

1. Download the required datasets.
2. Clean and preprocess the data.
3. Merge datasets using common geographic and temporal variables.
4. Engineer meaningful predictive features.
5. Develop and evaluate an AI or machine learning model.
6. Interpret model outputs and communicate findings.

---

## Suggested AI Applications

Participants may develop solutions such as:

* Malaria suitability mapping
* Cholera outbreak risk prediction
* Flood risk prediction
* Heatwave vulnerability assessment
* Environmental health decision-support systems

---

## Deliverables

Each team must submit:

* Public GitHub Repository
* Technical Report (Maximum 5 pages)
* Presentation Slides
* Five-minute Demonstration Video

The technical report should clearly describe:

* Data sources
* Data preprocessing
* Data integration
* Feature engineering
* Model development
* Model evaluation
* Limitations
* Public health implications

## Rules

* Only publicly available datasets should be used.
* All datasets must be properly cited.
* Additional open datasets may be incorporated provided their licenses permit reuse.
* All preprocessing, feature engineering, and modeling steps must be documented.
* Teams should ensure their work is reproducible.
